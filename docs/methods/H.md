# H: Is Slovenia's survey dip in the employment register? (round 3)

## 1. Question and registration
- **Question.** Eurostat's survey (LFS) shows Slovenia's share of ICT specialists falling from 4.8 % (2021) to 3.8 % (2023). Does the employment register, which counts everyone and has no sampling error, show a fall in IT employment too?
- **Prediction.** No: the register share of IT services (SKD J62 + J63) is higher in 2023 than in 2021 (d > 0).
- **Registration.** `docs/hypotheses/round3/H.md`, commit `c28fcb4 2026-11-25 15:35:34 +0100`; the SURS file was downloaded after it (15:36:18 the same day). Amendments: none.
- **Known before registering.** The survey values (rounds 1-2, B1). The register had never been downloaded. The primary was changed *before* registration, on simulated data only, from Newey-West to a simulated null (see section 4).

## 3. From raw data to the test sample
1. **Download.** SiStat table 0700921S as a PC-Axis file, parsed by `parse_px` in `collectors/surs.py`, line 50 into 240,240 rows (110 activities x 7 statuses x 312 months; 57,358 cells empty, none of them in the cells used).
2. **Cells used.** Status "1 Delovno aktivno prebivalstvo - SKUPAJ" (and "11 Zaposlene osebe - skupaj" for a secondary); activities total, J62, J63: 1,872 rows, no missing value.
3. **Share.** s(t) = 100 x (J62 + J63) / total, monthly (`share_series` in `analysis/hypotheses3_a.py`, line 41).
4. **Noise model.** Linear trend of s over 2015-01 to 2019-12 (60 months), AR(1) of the residuals: phi = 0.79, innovation sd = 0.0087 points (`ar1_noise` in `analysis/hypotheses3_a.py`, line 109).
5. **Test sample.** The 12 months of 2021 and the 12 of 2023.
6. **Check of a visible step.** The share jumps in January of most years (e.g. +0.03 points in 2021, 2022 and 2023): total employment dips every January (-0.9 % in 2023) while IT employment keeps growing (+0.45 % in January 2023, about the usual monthly rise). It is seasonal, not a reclassification.

## 4. The test
- **Primary.** d = mean(s, 2023) - mean(s, 2021). Null distribution: 10,000 simulated 36-month series of AR(1) noise with no change; two-sided p = (#|d_null| >= |d| + 1) / 10,001 (`sim_test` in `analysis/hypotheses3_a.py`, line 77). The smallest possible p is 0.0001.
- **Why not Newey-West.** In synthetic tests before registration, Newey-West with 24 strongly autocorrelated months rejected a true null 24 %, 31 % and 54 % of the time (phi 0.3, 0.6, 0.9); the simulated null keeps 5 %. Newey-West is reported only.
- **Secondary.** 2021 vs 2024: d = +0.332; J62 alone: +0.236; employees only: +0.211; with phi = 0.9: p = 0.0001; Newey-West: p = 2.5e-06 (not read as evidence); slowdown: 2022-2023 lies +0.107 points **above** the 2015-2019 trend (0.071 points a year), so no slowdown; persons in J62 + J63: 18,564 -> 21,475 (+15.7 %).
- **Placebo.** 2,000 series from random 12-month blocks of the real 2015-2019 residuals: 0.0 % with p < 0.05 (5 % expected; lower means the AR(1) model, if anything, overstates the noise, so the test is cautious).
- **Power (logged before d was computed).** Smallest shift found 80 % of the time: 0.039 points.
- **Multiple testing.** Round 3 family (G, H), Holm with m = 2: at most 2 x 0.0001 = 0.0002, so the verdict does not depend on G.

```python
def sim_test(s: pd.Series, phi: float, sd: float, base: int = BASE, target: int = TARGET, n_sim: int = 10_000, seed: int = 0) -> dict:
    """Primary test: observed d against its simulated null; two-sided p = (#|d_null| >= |d| + 1) / (n_sim + 1); 95 % interval d +- 1.96 sd(null)."""
    idx = pd.DatetimeIndex(s.index)
    a, b = s[idx.year == base], s[idx.year == target]
    if len(a) != 12 or len(b) != 12:
        raise ValueError(f"need 12 months in {base} and {target}, got {len(a)} and {len(b)}")
    d = float(b.mean() - a.mean())
    null = d_null(phi, sd, n_sim, seed)
    sdn = float(null.std(ddof=1))
    return {"n": 24, "mean_base": float(a.mean()), "mean_target": float(b.mean()), "d": d, "null_sd": sdn,
            "ci_lo": d - 1.96 * sdn, "ci_hi": d + 1.96 * sdn, "p": float(((np.abs(null) >= abs(d)).sum() + 1) / (n_sim + 1)), "n_sim": n_sim}
```

## 7. How to read it, and its limits
- *Po registru, ki šteje vse zaposlene, delež zaposlenih v IT podjetjih v Sloveniji med 2021 in 2023 ni padel, ampak zrasel (z 2,06 % na 2,30 %), ljudi pa je bilo 16 % več. Padec v anketi (s 4,8 % na 3,8 %) je zato najverjetneje posledica majhnega vzorca, ne pravega upada.*
- Limits: the register counts people **in IT firms** (including non-IT staff), the survey counts **IT specialists in any industry**; the two can differ for real. For the survey's fall to be real, IT specialists outside IT firms would have had to fall by roughly 9,000 to 12,000 in two years (the survey's -1.0 point of about 930,000 employed, plus whatever growth inside IT firms, which added about 2,900 people); possible, but unlikely. One country; no cause is tested.

## 8. Likely questions and answers
- **Why are the levels so different (2.3 % vs 4.8 %)?** Different things are counted: people working in IT companies vs. IT specialists anywhere (banks, industry, the state). The test compares changes, not levels.
- **So the survey is wrong?** We cannot say that; we can say the register shows no fall in IT employment, and that a fall of the size the survey shows would need a large, unseen loss of IT specialists outside IT firms.
- **Why a simulated null and not a regression?** With 24 monthly values that follow each other closely, the usual correction (Newey-West) finds differences that are not there up to half the time; we found this on simulated data before registering and changed the test then.
- **The jump in January 2023?** Seasonal: total employment dips every January; IT employment kept its usual growth.
- **Did Slovenian IT slow down after the rate rises, like in the US?** No: 2022-2023 was above the 2015-2019 trend.
