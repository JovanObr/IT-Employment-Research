## 3. From raw data to the test sample
1. **Events.** Tracker rows of companies matched to an SEC filer with a ticker: 691 rows, 686 unique company-days (`build_events` in `analysis/event_study.py`, line 88).
2. **Follow-ups dropped.** A later announcement of the same company within 30 days of the last kept one is dropped (it is the same news): **642 events** (`drop_clustered` in `analysis/event_study.py`, line 75).
3. **Returns.** Daily returns from adjusted prices; single-day moves beyond +-100 % are data glitches and blanked (100 returns in 22 tickers; none inside an announcement window) (`clean_returns` in `analysis/event_study.py`, line 55).
4. **Market model.** For each event, the stock's normal relation to the NASDAQ Composite (`^IXIC`) is estimated over trading days -250 to -30 (alpha, beta, residual spread) (`market_model` in `analysis/event_study.py`, line 109).
5. **Abnormal return.** Actual minus predicted return, summed over days 0 and +1 (day 0 = first trading day on or after the announcement) (`cars_at` in `analysis/event_study.py`, line 126).
6. **Usable events.** 31 lost for too little price history: **611 events** (`run_event_study` in `analysis/event_study.py`, line 144).
7. **Label.** Repeat = the company has an earlier tracker announcement on any earlier date (all tracker rows, also dropped follow-ups); first = none (`label_repeat` in `analysis/hypotheses.py`, line 129): **228 first, 383 repeat**.
8. **Winsorising.** Abnormal returns clipped at the 1st and 99th percentile over all events, so a few huge moves cannot decide the mean.

## 4. The test
- **Primary.** Difference in mean abnormal return (repeat - first); two-sided permutation test, labels shuffled across events 10,000 times; p = share of shuffled differences at least as large in absolute value (+1 correction).
- **Why.** Stock reactions have very fat tails (single events move 40-60 %); a permutation test needs no distributional assumption, because under "no difference" the labels are interchangeable. Means (not medians) to match how event studies, including the ones in notebook 09, report results.
- **Secondary.** Day-clustered bootstrap interval (events on the same day share market news): -0.38 to 2.62 points; Mann-Whitney on raw values, p = 0.009; without events before 2020-09-11: 1.29 points, p = 0.091 (identical to "without 2020": every 2020 event is before 2020-09-11).
- **Placebo.** Random non-event days of the same companies (at least 10 trading days from any real event), same labels, whole test repeated 100 times: false-positive rate **4 %**.
- **Power.** Minimum detectable difference 2.1 percentage points.
- **Multiple testing.** Round 1 family; Holm-adjusted p = **0.379**.

```python
def perm_diff(values: np.ndarray, labels: np.ndarray, n_perm: int = 10000, seed: int = 0) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """Mean(label 1) - mean(label 0) and its two-sided permutation p-value."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    obs = values[labels == 1].mean() - values[labels == 0].mean()
    n1 = int(labels.sum())
    perms = np.array([rng.permutation(values) for _ in range(n_perm)])
    null = perms[:, :n1].mean(axis=1) - perms[:, n1:].mean(axis=1)
    return float(obs), float((np.sum(np.abs(null) >= abs(obs)) + 1) / (n_perm + 1))
```

## 7. How to read it, and its limits
- *Borza se je na prvo napoved odpuščanj odzvala bolj (−1,31 %) kot na ponovljene (−0,14 %), torej nasprotno od pričakovanja, a razlika ni značilna.
  Verjetna razlaga: prva napoved je novica, naslednje vlagatelji že pričakujejo.*
- Limits: "first" means first in the tracker (from March 2020), not first ever; repeat announcers may differ in other ways (size, sector).

## 8. Likely questions and answers
- **What is an abnormal return?** The part of a price move the overall market does not explain, from each stock's own normal relation to the NASDAQ.
- **Why the NASDAQ as the market?** Most matched companies are tech; the S&P 500 gave the same picture in notebook 08's robustness checks.
- **Why winsorise?** A handful of moves of 40-60 % (often unrelated news) would decide a mean of 611 events.
- **The secondary test is significant (p = 0.009): why is the verdict "not supported"?** Only the primary test decides; and it points the opposite way from the prediction.
- **Could repeat announcers simply be bigger companies?** Possibly; the test does not control for it. That is listed as a limit and a question for a new hypothesis.
