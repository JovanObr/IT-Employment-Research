## 1. Question and registration
- **Question.** Do US tech companies that announce a layoff in year t have a larger fall in employees from end of t-1 to end of t, for t = 2024 and 2025 pooled?
- **Prediction.** Negative difference (announcers shrink more).
- **Registration.** `docs/hypotheses/round2/C2_E.md`, commit `ad835e5 2026-11-20 11:58:03 +0100`. The 2024 and 2025 headcounts were downloaded afterwards (file written 2026-11-20 11:28 UTC; registration committed 10:58 UTC). Amendments: none.
- **Known before registering.** Round 1's exploratory result (2022 -> 2023: announcers -7 %, p < 0.001). It used 2022-2023 data; C2 uses only 2023-2025 headcounts and 2024-2025 announcements, so it is a genuine replication on data that did not suggest the hypothesis.

## 4. The test
- **Primary.** Difference in medians (announced - not), pooled over both years; two-sided permutation test shuffling the announced label **within each year** (10,000 permutations, group sizes per year kept). 95 % interval from 5,000 bootstrap resamples of companies (all of a company's years together).
- **Why.** Heavy tails, as in C, so medians and a permutation test. Shuffling within year means a difference between 2024 and 2025 (more announcers and slower growth in one year) cannot create an effect; a synthetic test checks exactly this (`test_c2_year_difference_alone_does_not_create_an_effect`). Resampling whole companies keeps their two years together.
- **Secondary.** Each year alone; Mann-Whitney on the pooled sample, p = 0.0020; OLS with log assets, SIC 3-digit and year dummies, errors clustered by company: -0.082 (-0.124 to -0.041), p = 0.00011.
- **Placebo.** Labels shuffled within year, whole test repeated 500 times: false-positive rate **4.4 %**.
- **Power (computed before the comparison).** Minimum detectable shift 0.092 log points.
- **Multiple testing.** Round 2 family A2, F, C2, E. Holm multiplies a p-value by at most 4, so p = 0.0009 stays below 0.05 whatever the other three give.

```python
def perm_within(y: np.ndarray, lab: np.ndarray, strata: np.ndarray, n_perm: int, rng) -> np.ndarray:
    """Null distribution of the difference in medians, labels shuffled within each stratum (group sizes per stratum kept)."""
    idx = [np.flatnonzero(strata == s) for s in np.unique(strata)]
    out = np.empty(n_perm)
    for k in range(n_perm):
        l2 = lab.copy()
        for i in idx:
            l2[i] = rng.permutation(lab[i])
        out[k] = _median_diff(y, l2)
    return out
```

- *Podjetja, ki so v letu 2024 ali 2025 napovedala odpuščanja, so v istem letu zmanjšala število zaposlenih (v srednjem −4 %), ostala pa so ga
  rahlo povečala (+1 %). Napovedi iz našega seznama torej res pomenijo manj zaposlenih, kar smo zdaj potrdili na podatkih, ki jih prej nismo videli.*
- Limits: the upper end of the interval is almost zero (-0.0001), so the size of the effect is uncertain even if its direction is clear; same measurement limits as C.

## 8. Likely questions and answers
- **Isn't this just round 1 again?** No: same question, but different years (2024-2025) that played no part in suggesting it; registered before the 2024-2025 headcounts were downloaded.
- **Why pool two years?** One year alone had too little power (48 announcers); the registration says so and fixed the pooling in advance.
- **Why shuffle within year?** So that a difference between the years (more layoffs and slower growth in one of them) cannot pass for an effect.
- **The interval nearly touches zero: is it really significant?** The permutation p is 0.0009 and both secondary tests agree; the bootstrap interval is wider because it resamples whole companies. The direction is solid; the exact size is uncertain.
- **Does this prove the tracker counts are accurate?** It shows announcements go with real headcount falls; it does not check the number of people in each announcement.
