## 1. Question and registration
- **Question.** Across the 27 EU countries, is the change in the share of ICT specialists in total employment from 2021 to 2024 negatively
  related to the country's level before the period (catch-up)?
- **Prediction.** Negative slope (b < 0).
- **Registration.** `docs/hypotheses/round2/A2_F.md`, section "A2", commit `fdd6e00 2026-11-23 16:42:14 +0100`. Code and synthetic tests
  committed afterwards and before the real run: `e79b61e 2026-11-23 16:47:29 +0100`. Results: `7ac6b78 2026-11-23 16:50:06 +0100`.
- **Amendments.** None.
- **Known before registering (copied from the disclosure).** A2 comes from round 1's A: every country's change 2021 -> 2024 had been computed
  and seen (Slovenia -0.50 points, rank 27 of 27; mean of the other 26 +0.58). The relation between those changes and any start level was not
  computed or plotted, and the 2019-2020 levels of the other countries were not looked at for this question. **This makes A2 a weaker
  confirmation than a test on new data**: the outcome values were known, only their relation to the start level was not.

## 4. The test
- **Primary.** OLS slope b of change_c on start_c; two-sided permutation p with 10,000 permutations of change_c across countries,
  p = (#|b_perm| >= |b_obs| + 1) / 10,001; 95 % interval from 5,000 bootstrap resamples of countries (percentile). Alpha 0.05 after Holm.
- **Why a 2019-2020 start, not 2021 (regression to the mean).** If the start were share(2021), the same noisy 2021 number would enter the start
  with + and the change with -, so a negative slope would appear even with no real catch-up. 2019 and 2020 are different survey years from
  2021 and 2024, so independent sampling noise cannot create a slope. The 2021 method break sits between start and change: a country-specific
  level shift at the break moves the start relative to the new series but does not enter the change, which is measured wholly in the new series.
- **Why a permutation test.** With 27 points one extreme country (Sweden, Finland, Luxembourg have high levels) could distort a normal-theory
  p-value; the permutation test needs only exchangeability under no relation. The classical OLS p is reported next to it (0.732 vs. 0.734).
- **Assumptions checked.** Linearity is not needed for the permutation test to be valid (it tests "no relation"); its power is for a linear
  relation. The bootstrap needs at least 3 distinct start levels per resample (always true here, 5,000 of 5,000 kept).
- **Secondary** (cannot change the verdict): naive 2021 start (shows the regression-to-the-mean bias next to the primary); Spearman rank
  correlation; relative change; 2021 -> 2025; without Slovenia.
- **Placebo (simulation with the measured noise).** sigma = root mean square over the 27 countries of the residual sd of a linear trend fitted
  to each country's 2015-2020 values: **sigma = 0.183 points**. Simulated world without catch-up: true level = observed start, true change
  drawn independently of the level from N(0.541, 0.415) (mean and sd of the observed changes, sd corrected for the noise), every yearly value
  plus N(0, sigma). 2,000 data sets, 1,000 permutations each. False-positive rate: **4.55 %** with the registered design, **5.1 %** with the
  naive 2021 start (mean naive slope -0.018). So with this amount of noise regression to the mean would have been small; the registered
  design is calibrated.
- **Power.** n = 27: |r| of about 0.52 is detectable with 80 % power at alpha 0.05 (Fisher z, 2.80 / sqrt(24)). Computed from n alone.
- **Multiple testing.** Round 2 family A2, F, C2, E, Holm. Raw p: E 0.0002, C2 0.0009, F 0.292, A2 0.734. Holm-adjusted A2: **0.734**.

```python
def perm_slope_p(x: np.ndarray, y: np.ndarray, n_perm: int, rng) -> tuple[float, np.ndarray]:
    """Two-sided permutation p of the OLS slope: y shuffled across units; p = (#|b_perm| >= |b_obs| + 1) / (n_perm + 1)."""
    x, y = np.asarray(x, float), np.asarray(y, float)
    xc = x - x.mean()
    obs = float(xc @ (y - y.mean()) / (xc @ xc))
    Y = np.array([rng.permutation(y) for _ in range(n_perm)])
    null = (Y - Y.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)) @ xc / (xc @ xc)
    return float((np.sum(np.abs(null) >= abs(obs) - 1e-12) + 1) / (n_perm + 1)), null
```

- **Unexpected.** The naive design is almost unbiased here (placebo 5.1 %): the year-to-year noise in this series is small relative to the
  real differences between countries.

## 7. How to read it, and its limits
- *Države EU, ki so imele pred letom 2021 več strokovnjakov IKT, med 2021 in 2024 niso rasle opazno počasneje: naklon je skoraj nič. Slovenija je
  začela na sredini lestvice, zato bi po skupnem trendu pričakovali rast za okoli pol odstotne točke; namesto tega je njen delež padel.
  Slovenskega rezultata torej ne pojasni visoko izhodišče.*
- Limits: 27 countries detect only strong relations (|r| about 0.5); the interval still allows a modest catch-up (down to -0.20 points per point
  of start). Survey estimates in small countries (SI, CY, LU, MT) are noisy. The change outcome was already known from round 1. A
  correlation across countries says nothing about why.

## 8. Likely questions and answers
- **Why not just regress the change on the 2021 level?** Then the same noisy 2021 value is on both sides and a negative slope appears even
  without catch-up (regression to the mean). We used 2019-2020 and showed by simulation what the naive design would do.
- **Is the data normalised?** Yes: the share divides ICT specialists by all employed persons, so country size drops out. It does not remove the
  larger sampling error of small countries.
- **The 2021 break: does the start level come from a different method?** Yes. That is deliberate: the change is measured wholly in the new
  method; a level shift at the break moves the start, not the change. If the break shifted countries very differently, the start would be a
  noisier measure of the true level, which weakens the test (attenuation) but cannot create a slope.
- **Isn't this circular, since you had already seen the changes?** Partly; that is why the dossier calls it a weaker confirmation. The relation to
  the start level was not computed before registering, and the result came out null, the opposite of what the round 1 story suggested.
- **Why is Slovenia's p 0.029 not a finding?** It is descriptive, uncorrected, and essentially round 1's A again (that test was not supported
  after Holm). It answers the reviewer's question only in one direction: the start level does not explain the drop.
- **What would explain Slovenia then?** Not tested. The B1 exploratory figures show that 2021 was Slovenia's highest year, the lowest was 2023,
  and the share rose again in 2024-2025; the choice of 2021 as the first year matters (see `docs/methods/A.md`).
